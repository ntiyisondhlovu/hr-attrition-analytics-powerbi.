
# Insights and recommendations

All figures come from the IBM HR Employee Attrition dataset (1,470 employees, 237 leavers, 16.1% overall attrition). The data is synthetic, so treat these as a demonstration of method.

## Key findings
1. Overtime is the strongest driver. Employees working overtime leave at 30.5%, against 10.4% for those who do not. They account for 54% of all leavers. If their rate matched the rest, about 84 fewer people would leave and the overall rate would fall to about 10.4%. This is an illustration, not a forecast.
2. Risk is concentrated at entry level. Job Level 1 makes up 37% of staff and 60% of leavers (143 of 237), with a 26.3% attrition rate.
3. Combined factors are worse. Overtime plus a bottom-quartile income shows a 58.5% rate (group of 106 employees). Overtime plus 2 years or less of tenure shows 51% (group of 104).
4. Early tenure and youth matter. First-year employees leave at about 35%, falling to about 8% after 10 years. Under-25s leave at about 36%.
5. Rate and volume tell different stories. Sales Representatives have the highest rate (about 40%, 33 leavers). Laboratory Technicians have the most leavers (62, at about 24%).
6. Pay and equity relate to attrition. The lowest income quartile leaves at about 29%, against about 10% in the top quartile. Employees with no stock options leave at about 24%, against 8-9% for levels 1 and 2.
7. High performers leave at the same rate as others: 16.4% for rating 4, against 16.1% for rating 3.

## Recommendations
- Review overtime and workload in Level 1 and low-income roles.
- Strengthen first-year onboarding and check-ins.
- Review pay in the bottom income band and the use of stock options.
- Target retention actions at Sales Representatives and Laboratory Technicians.
- Look at why high performers are not retained better than others.

## Limitations
- Synthetic data with no date field, so no trends over time.
- Findings are associations, not causes. Overtime overlaps with role, level, and income.
- Some groups are small, so group sizes should be read alongside every rate.
